### A6 · Decades-Long Alleged Corruption at Rabinovich & Gesher (English)
**URL:** filmindustrywatch.org/israel-decades-long-alleged-corruption-at-the-rabinowitz-gesher-film-funds/
**Date:** April 1, 2024
**Category:** Alleged Conflict of Interest + Financial Misconduct

#### What the article claims (summary — full article is ~9,700 words)

**Key figures:**
- **Giora Eini** (גיורא עיני) — Rabinovich Fund CEO since 1995; connections to Yitzhak Rabin and Shimon Peres; alleged quote: "I only care about the money."
- **Yoav Abramovich** — Artistic Director, Rabinovich Fund; present in 26 project applications (allegedly as "independent" reader — against the rules).
- **Eti Cohen** — Film Department Director, Ministry of Culture 1999–2021; alleged romantic relationship with Eini; alleged insider information sharing; allegedly omitted her name from council meeting records.
- **Moshe Edery** — producer + owner of UnitedKing distribution + owner of Cinema City chain: a **vertical monopoly** in production, distribution, and exhibition.
- **Ziv Nave** — Gesher CEO; alleged deal with Minister Hili Trooper (whose father founded Gesher); allegedly met annually with Eini and Cohen at Cannes to plan the following year's funding.

**Key financial claims:**
- 2015–2021: Rabinovich + IFF invested **₪261,347,115 total** in feature films.
- Moshe Edery received **26,376,655 EUR** for 85 films (35% of total).
- **49% of Rabinovich Fund** went to Edery-produced films.
- 37% of all film investments went to UnitedKing ecosystem.
- 2012–2016: 99 of 464 funded films (21%) had **no reader scoring**.
- Edery brothers involved in 24 of those 99 no-score films.
- Score manipulation: one project with 8.8/10 average across 10 reviews received 5/10 from a Rabinovich reader — allegedly to sabotage it.

**Film Council conflicts (detailed list):**
- 2 council members received funding while in office: Yair Rave (יאיר רוה, "Kathmandu" series + "Sharon Amarni" film), Arnon Zadok (ארנון צדוק, "Nika" film).
- 3 council members employed by funds: Eve Divon, Merlin Venig, Rabbi Israel Galis (all at Gesher incubator 2012).
- 6 council members appeared in funded films: **Osnat Bukofzer**, Noam Shanhav, Golan Azulai, **Aryeh Cherner** (12 films), + others.
- 1 council member (unnamed) became Rabinovich reader after term.

**Gesher-specific claims:**
- At Gesher: artistic consultant present in **72%** of funded applications.
- At Rabinovich: CEO/Artistic Director present in **45%** of funded applications.
- **Sharon Shamir** present in 6 Gesher applications (against rules).
- **Oren Reich** — worked at Yehuda+Samaria Fund + appeared on Gesher website.
- 2014 Supreme Court case against Eini regarding legislative amendment (not blocked).
- Criteria changes in 2014 and 2019 → Rabinovich funding +9% / +29%; Gesher funding +149% / +82%.

### Cross-Cutting Themes

**1. Structural insider loop (confirmed pattern)**
The same mechanism repeats across all articles: persons who serve on selection committees, fund boards, or juries also receive funding, are employed by the funds they regulate, or have family/business partners who benefit. This is documented with varying degrees of evidence across A1, A5, A6, A12.

**2. Financial concentration (partially verified, needs Guidestar)**
The 49% / 37% figures in A6 are the strongest financial claim and would, if verified, represent a significant finding. The article provides a breakdown methodology and screenshots — but the primary source data (annual reports) is not shown.

**3. Political-level intervention (recent and documented)**
A9 and A13 describe post-2024 government intervention that is well-sourced in Israeli mainstream press. This layer (Zohar's reforms, Ophir defunding) is distinct from and layered on top of the fund-level corruption described in A1/A5/A6.

**4. Gender politics as a separate axis (FIW's editorial framing)**
A3, A10, A12 (and to some extent A11) push a "radical left / DEI overreach" narrative that is the site's editorial framing, not just neutral fact reporting. The statistical data (Ophir 11/12 female nominations; JFL 4/4 female winners) is factual but the interpretation is contested. These articles mix valid conflict-of-interest observations (Eti Tsicko on programming committee; Sam Spiegel closed loop) with editorializing about gender politics. Treat the COI claims as verifiable; treat the "discrimination against men" framing as the source's editorial opinion.

**5. Festival circuit as echo chamber (partially verified)**
A2 (Welinski/Cannes) and A12 (Sam Spiegel/JFF) describe a closed network where festival programmers are also producers of competing films, and where school alumni get privileged access. A2's specific claims are well documented with public sources. A12's Tsicko-committee claim needs verification.
