Background: 
The history of Jewish communities in the lands of Islam constitutes a rich and 
significant chapter in the story of the Jewish people. Viewed through the lens of the 
centuries that followed the rise of Islam in the 7th century up to the beginning of the 
20th century, what emerges is a vivid portrait of thriving Jewish communities across 
North Africa, the Middle East, the Ottoman Empire and Asia. As Islam spread rapidly 
across the region and beyond, the Jewish world began to respond, laying the 
foundations for the evolving relationship between Jews and Muslims. 
 
Only in recent years has this chapter of Jewish history - centered in the lands of Islam 
- begun to receive broader recognition. Stories, communities, key figures and lived 
experiences that were long marginalized, forgotten, or insufficiently acknowledged, 
are gradually being integrated into the larger historical and cultural narrative. 
Connecting with this past through new media opens up new possibilities for telling the 
stories of these communities in ways that are resonant, immersive, and relevant 
today.

Additionally, proposals may include immersive video installations designed for 
exhibition spaces in which the experience is projected onto all walls and viewed 
without mediation devices. 
 
The visual language and expressive tools may include 360° live-action or documentary 
video, animation, graphics and design, or a combination of visual, sonic, and haptic 
elements. Use of AI-based tools is also welcome. 
 
Immersive and interactive experiences offer new ways to represent and recreate the 
past. They allow us to deconstruct and reassemble the lives – social, religious, and 
cultural experiences of individuals and communities – as well as the experience of 
immigration and displacement. Immersive environments allow narratives to 
“breathe” and invite the viewer to become an active participant in an emotional, 
cultural, and historical journey. These experiences help shape compelling and 
resonant narratives, fostering deeper identification, empathy, and historical insight.

Open Call Objectives: 
This open call aims to support the development and production of new media works 
that tell the stories of Jewish communities in the Islamic countries, with an emphasis 
on narratives that demonstrate a clear storytelling arc and a distinctive, innovative 
artistic language. Creators are expected to select a story, character(s), event and 
historical period that reflect the uniqueness and identity of a specific community, 
culture, time and place. The thematic scope spans nearly 1,500 years and 
encompasses a wide geographical range, offering numerous directions for exploration 
and focus. 
 
Unlike the goals of the documentary series being produced in parallel - which aims to 
provide a broad and comprehensive historical overview - applicants to this open call 
are expected to create intimate, immersive experiences, amplified by the 
technologies and expressive languages unique to new media narratives. Submitted 
projects must focus on characters, events, or communities that existed between the 
early 7th century (around the rise of Islam) and the year 1900.  
 
In addition, the creators, who will receive production grants from the foundation 
and partners, will also be asked to prepare a short film of approximately 8-12 
minutes in length that will be based on the proposed content. The film is intended 
to be uploaded on Kan's platforms to promote the documentary series and 
immersive experiences. At the submission stage, there is no need to provide details 
about the content of the video, but to focus solely on the immersive experience. The 
content of the video will be determined at a later date between the creators and the 
project funding partners. 
 
For inspiration and to better understand the historical context and spirit of the time 
and place, applicants may refer to the summary of the documentary series included 
below (without section Four, which is about the 20th century). This overview is 
intended for background purposes only and does not require any narrative or 
conceptual overlap between the series and the proposed immersive projects.

Open Call Process and Timeline: 
In the first stage, 6-8 projects will be selected from all eligible applications. Each 
proposal must be submitted by a team consisting of a creator (writer/director) and a 
creative technologist (see eligibility details below). The selected projects will 
participate in a professional incubator workshop in Tel Aviv in November 2025 (full 
schedule follows). The selected teams are required to participate and be physically 
present throughout the entire workshop. 
 
The purpose of the workshop is to provide guidance, support and mentorship from 
leading professionals in the fields of screenwriting, new media, animation and 
technology. In addition, teams will receive support in developing an impact strategy 
for production and distribution. The goal is to refine and improve the projects in all 
aspects, including narrative, content, script, visual language, creative expression, 
production and marketing/distribution plans, and to ensure that each project aligns 
with a realistic budget.  
 
As part of the workshop activities in November, an Open Industry Day will be held in 
which all selected teams are required to attend. 
 
At the conclusion of the Workshop, and following the submission of final proposals, 3 
projects will be selected to receive funding from Makor Foundation, with the support 
of Mifal HaPais-Israel State Lottery and other partners. Each selected project will 
receive an investment of approximately 433,000 NIS for production. 
 
To streamline production costs and meet the exhibition timeline, the three selected 
projects will be produced in collaboration with an independent production company 
experienced in XR productions, to be appointed at a later stage. The terms of 
engagement between the creators and the production company will be defined 
following the selection of the projects. 
 
The three selected teams will also participate in the XR co-production market at the 
NewImages Festival in Paris, in Spring 2026. This visit is intended to expose the 
projects to international producers, distributors, creators, institutions and potential 
partners. Participation in the festival is mandatory and requires the presence and full 
participation of both members of the creative team. The creators of the selected 
projects will receive partial funding to cover participation expenses at the festival. If

Overview of the 10-parts documentary series, without section Four, which is 
about the 20th century (for inspiration only) 
 
Section One – The Rise of Muhammad and the Jewish Golden Age 
After the destruction of the Second Temple, Jewish communities were dispersed, 
distinct and separate. Muhammad’s birth and the rise of Islam swept the region. 
Jewish communities’ varied responses shaped Jewish-Arab relations and led to the 
Jewish Golden Age in Spain. This period leads to the Golden Age in Spain, the 
development and consolidation of the Jewish language and literary canon, and 
ultimately to the expulsion from Spain. 
Period: From the rise of Islam (~700 CE) through the Middle Ages to the expulsion from 
Spain (1492) and Safed (15th century). Regions: Arabian Peninsula, Babylonia, Spain, 
North Africa. 
 
Section Two – The Aftershocks of the Expulsion from Spain 
The expulsion flooded the Ottoman Arab world with Jewish refugees from Spain and 
Portugal. Cultural, religious, and economic differences between the Sephardic 
refugees and the established Musta'arabi1 communities led to leadership struggles 
and tensions between rivalry and integration efforts. Ottoman religious autonomy 
redefined relations with Muslims and inspired messianic creativity. Waves of 
prosperity and hardship affected material culture and caused disputes over identity 
and Jewish law amid existential survival struggles. The printing press and scientific 
revolution challenged traditional ways of life. 
Period: 15th–18th century. Regions: Ottoman Empire, North Africa, Persia, Yemen, 
Bukhara. 
 
Section Three – Colonialism and Nationalism: Welcome to Modernity 
New communication and travel routes connected Jewish communities globally, 
creating a transnational Jewish consciousness with internal hierarchies. The Ottoman 
Empire’s collapse and colonialism introduced new identities and tensions between 
Jews of Islam and European Jews. The establishment of Israel and the disappearance 
of Jewish presence in Muslim lands erased many of these possibilities. 
Period: 18th century–1948. Regions: Ottoman Empire, North Africa, Iraq, Yemen. 
 
1 (Arabic:   اﻟﻤﺴﺘﻌᖁᗖني نal-Mustaʿribīn "Mozarabs") were the Arabic-speaking Jews, largely Mizrahi Jews 
and Maghrebi Jews, who lived in the Middle East and North Africa.
