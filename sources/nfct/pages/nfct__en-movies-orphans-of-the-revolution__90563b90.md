German\n\n\n![](https://nfct.org.il/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2015/09/orphans.jpg)\n“A family’s story is the starting point for a discussion of the moral and political ideas (and clichés) that shook Europe and the Middle East throughout the 20th Century and still reverberate today.  \nThe story begins in the 1930s in the Warsaw Pawiak Prison where Igal Bursztyn’s mother was incarcerated for illegal Communist activities. It ends in an Israeli jail on the West Bank, where Bursztyn himself served as a jailor to Palestinian prisoners as part of his reserve duty. How did my mother’s son become a jailor? “\n**Festivals and Awards:**\nThe Haifa International Film Festival, Israel, 2015\n**Director** : Igal Bursztyn\n**Producer** : Igal Bursztyn\n**Cinematographer** : Ron Katznelson, Andrzej Wojciechowski\n**Editor** : Era Lapid\n**Soundtrack Editor** : Gil Toren\n**Supporter(s)** : The New Fund for Cinema and Television, Channel 1 - Israel Broadcasting Authority\n**Musicians** : Schubert\n**Script** : Igal Bursztyn\n**Subtitles** : English, Hebrew\n**Film Name in Hebrew** : [יתומי המהפכה](https://nfct.org.il/blog/movies/%D7%99%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%99-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%9B%D7%94/)\n**Category** : [Family](https://nfct.org.il/en/movies-archive?category=Family) / [History](https://nfct.org.il/en/movies-archive?category=History) / [Identity](https://nfct.org.il/en/movies-archive?category=Identity) / [Israeli-Palestinian Conflict](https://nfct.org.il/en/movies-archive?category=Israeli-Palestinian%20Conflict)\n**Tag Words** : [historical events](https://nfct.org.il/en/movies-archive?keywords=historical%20events) [hope](https://nfct.org.il/en/movies-archive?keywords=hope) [jail](https://nfct.org.il/en/movies-archive?keywords=jail)\n**Share** : 003\n“A family’s story is the starting point for a discussion of the moral and political ideas (and clichés) that shook Europe and the Middle East throughout the 20th Century and still reverberate today.  \nThe story begins in the 1930s in the Warsaw Pawiak Prison where Igal Bursztyn’s mother was incarcerated for illegal Communist activities.
